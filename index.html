Election night

Night

Polls close across six time zones on a 10-minute clock. Some states report nearly everything within hours, others take days. Early, mail and Election Day votes arrive in different orders.

How it works

  • Campaign. Build a candidate, then take a short political test whose answers become your platform. Pick a race and run it week by week: choose your message, where you campaign, which voters you court, how you spend your days and money. Media and social tabs show how the story is going. The result plays out on election night.
  • Election night. Votes are counted county by county with realistic reporting patterns. A call desk projects winners only when a race is out of reach. The national mood sets the tide; candidates move individual races, usually by a point or two.
  • Real data. Baseline margins use real 2024 presidential results and 2026 results as of Nov 25, 2026 (unofficial). County outlines are real. Counties in 18 states use real 2026 returns; others use 2024 county results. Candidate names for 2026 come from public sources and may contain errors. House names are nominees, and independents appear only as "other".
  • Simulated. Polls, news outlets, social platforms and storylines are simulated. Outlets and platforms are fictional; storylines marked "Real" follow actual 2026 events. Poll closing times, counting speed and each candidate's hidden traits are modeled. 2028 is hypothetical.
  • Known limits. Independents cannot win on election night. House districts have no outline map. Connecticut, Rhode Island, Alaska and DC report by town or district, so they show boxes instead of county shapes. Balance is still rough.
